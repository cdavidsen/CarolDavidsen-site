Cost of Search Carol Davidsen

About

Carol Davidsen has spent two decades inside the systems that decide what people see, pay, and are targeted with, moving from telecom billing to audience measurement and privacy to enterprise customer data architecture. She built the targeting platform that let the 2012 Obama campaign microtarget television advertising down to the household, then spent the next decade building the governed customer data infrastructure that lets large, regulated institutions actually know their own customers instead of guessing. The thread running through both is not a single tool. It is a pattern: translate a business problem into a data architecture, evangelize and sell the case for it to the people who have to fund it, then lead the engineers who design and ship it. Use case and deployment, pitch and platform, in roughly equal measure, which is rarer than either skill alone.

She is a native New Yorker and the parent of a ten-year-old, a personal stake that sharpens how she looks at this world: not just the technology, but the revenue a data product generates, the billing and licensing that funds it, and the regulatory and audit trail that has to justify it after the fact.
